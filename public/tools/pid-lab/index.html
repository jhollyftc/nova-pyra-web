How the loop works

Every millisecond the controller reads the encoder, compares it with the setpoint, and sets the motor voltage. The difference is the error, e.

P · present

Kp·e. Big error, big push. Alone it leaves a small offset whenever a steady load (like gravity) needs a constant voltage to hold.

I · past

Ki·∫e. Keeps growing while any error remains, so it finds the holding voltage by itself. Slow to react, and it can overshoot.

D · future

Kd·de/dt. Opposes fast change, acting like a shock absorber. Reduces overshoot, but noisy sensors make it jittery.

Model: J·θ̈ = 50·u − 2·θ̇ − load − disturbance (rad/s², u in volts, clamped to ±12 V). The controller and physics run at 1 kHz with a 10 ms derivative filter. Units are scaled for teaching, not a specific motor datasheet.
