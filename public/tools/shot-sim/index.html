Target geometry
The two HIVEs sit 25.5 in apart across the opening, not downrange of it — confirmed against the CAD plan view using the frame's own 49.46 × 38.95 in bounding box as the scale. So there is a full 72 in of floor in front of the opening and the FIELD is off-centre sideways by 12.75 in.

CELL opening 20 × 14 in, plane tilted 30° from vertical, bottom lip 53.5 in and top lip 65.6 in above the TILES (Figure 9-10). Bottom of HIVE 30.6 in per Team Update 01, 17 Sep 2026. Modelling the 14 in opening at 30° reproduces both lip heights to 0.01 in, which is what validates the tilt.
Ball data
POLLEN 2.8 in / 25 g, NECTAR 3.6 in / 40 g. Diameters are from the manual (Sec 9.8); the masses are team-measured — the manual states no weight for either ball. They give near-identical drag parameters (0.0457 vs 0.0473 m⁻¹), so the two balls fly almost the same arc.
The entry cone is the load-bearing assumption
A ball counts as scoring if it crosses the opening within 60° of square. Sweeping that from 45° to 70° moves the best fixed hood angle from 66° to 80° and coverage from 59% to 100%, so treat the fixed-angle recommendation as provisional until you have thrown real balls at a real CELL. The slider is there to be corrected.
What this model ignores
No spin or Magnus force, no bounce-in — the ball has to fly cleanly through the opening, and its full diameter has to clear all four lips along the way, not merely at the instant its centre crosses the plane. The only obstacle checked is the opening plane itself, so a shot that would clip the frame or the downward CELL can still read as a score. Air density 1.225 kg/m³. Treat it as a design envelope, not a guarantee.
The rpm figure is a floor
Ball speed is converted to wheel speed geometrically and nothing else: rpm = v / (πd) × 60, doubled for a single wheel because a ball pinched between one wheel and a stationary hood leaves at half the surface speed — the rest becomes backspin. Two counter-rotating wheels give the full surface speed. That arrangement is worth a factor of two, so check the toggle matches your design before trusting the number. No slip, no compression loss and no spin-down between shots are modelled, so a real launcher needs more than this — commonly 15–40% more. Size a motor and a ratio from it, then find the real setpoint by shooting.
